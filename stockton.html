How Common Are Serious Accidents in Stockton?

Traffic collisions remain a significant source of serious injuries in Stockton. According to the California Office of Traffic Safety's 2023 data, 1,913 people were killed or injured in traffic collisions in Stockton, ranking 3rd out of 15 similarly sized California cities.

The surrounding area also sees substantial collision activity. San Joaquin County recorded 6,095 people killed or injured in traffic collisions in 2023, including 771 in alcohol-involved crashes and 275 pedestrians. While these numbers do not establish who was responsible for any individual accident, they show how often serious crashes affect people throughout Stockton and San Joaquin County.

Who Can Be Held Liable for a Personal Injury in Stockton?

Because accidents happen for many reasons, determining liability can be a complex and time-consuming process. In every personal injury case, liability depends on why the injury occurred and who caused it. An injured person must establish that another party owed them a duty of care, breached that duty, and caused injuries and damages as a result.

Depending on the accident, potentially liable parties may include:

  • A negligent driver
  • A commercial truck driver or trucking company
  • An employer whose employee caused an accident while working
  • A property owner or business
  • A landlord or property management company
  • A manufacturer or distributor of a defective product
  • A dog owner
  • A rideshare or delivery driver
  • A government entity responsible for a dangerous public condition

Identifying the correct defendant matters because the responsible party is not always obvious right after an accident. For example, the California Courts note that personal injury lawsuits can involve the person who caused the injury, the owner of a vehicle or property, or an employer if the responsible person was working at the time.

What if More Than One Person Caused My Injury?

If more than one party caused the injury, California allows fault to be shared under its pure comparative negligence system. Even if you were partially responsible for the accident, you can still recover compensation, but your recovery will be reduced by your percentage of responsibility.

For example, if someone suffers $200,000 in damages but is found 20% responsible for the accident, their recovery could be reduced to $160,000.

Because of how comparative negligence works, insurance companies may investigate whether the injured person did anything that contributed to the accident in order to lower what they pay.

What Should I Do After an Accident in Stockton?

What you do immediately after an accident can affect both your health and a potential personal injury claim. When possible, an injured person should:

  • Seek medical attention: Some injuries may not produce noticeable symptoms immediately after an accident.
  • Report the accident: Depending on what happened, this may involve law enforcement, a property owner, an employer, or a business.
  • Photograph the scene: Capture vehicles, property conditions, injuries, road conditions, debris, traffic signals, and anything else relevant.
  • Collect witness information: Independent witnesses can become important when liability is disputed.
  • Preserve documents: Keep medical records, bills, receipts, repair estimates, insurance correspondence, and employment records.
  • Avoid discussing fault prematurely: The cause of an accident may require further investigation.
  • Be cautious when speaking with insurers: Insurance adjusters may request recorded statements or documents shortly after an accident.
  • Consider speaking with a personal injury attorney: An attorney can evaluate potential defendants, insurance coverage, evidence, and applicable filing deadlines.

What Evidence Can Help Prove a Stockton Personal Injury Claim?

Strong personal injury claims are generally supported by evidence that connects another party's conduct to the injury and documents the resulting damages. Useful evidence may include:

  • Police or collision reports
  • Photographs and videos
  • Dashcam footage
  • Traffic camera footage
  • Witness statements
  • Medical records
  • Social media posts
  • Vehicle damage
  • Cell phone records
  • Employment and wage records
  • Property maintenance records
  • Inspection reports
  • Commercial driver records
  • Expert analysis

The evidence you need will vary significantly by case. A slip and fall claim may depend heavily on surveillance footage, inspection procedures, and maintenance records, while a commercial truck accident claim could involve driver logs, vehicle maintenance records, electronic control module data, and company policies.

What Compensation Can I Recover After an Injury?

A California personal injury claim may seek compensation for both financial and non-financial losses caused by the accident. While every accident is different, potential damages can include medical expenses, lost income, pain and suffering, emotional distress, property damage, and even wrongful death damages. The value of every case will vary based on its details and the available evidence.

How Much Is a Stockton Personal Injury Case Worth?

There is no standard settlement amount for a personal injury claim, because its value depends on the specific facts of the accident and the resulting injuries. Relevant factors may include:

  • Severity of the injuries
  • Cost of medical treatment
  • Whether future medical care is necessary
  • Time missed from work
  • Reduced earning capacity
  • Whether injuries are permanent
  • Available insurance coverage
  • Strength of the liability evidence
  • Whether multiple parties share responsibility
  • The injured person's percentage of fault
  • How the injury affects daily life

A good rule of thumb is whether the accident caused severe or permanent injury. A relatively minor collision resulting in temporary soft-tissue injuries will be valued lower than an accident causing spinal cord damage, traumatic brain injury, amputation, or another permanent disability.

While personal injury settlement calculators can provide rough estimates, a skilled personal injury lawyer can give you a more accurate picture of what you may actually recover.

What if the Insurance Company Says I Caused the Accident?

If an insurance company says you caused the accident, don't panic. Its determination does not decide legal responsibility. Insurers rely on driver statements, police reports, photographs, vehicle damage, witnesses, and other evidence when evaluating liability, and additional evidence can change how an accident is understood. A personal injury lawyer can gather that evidence and challenge the degree of fault an insurance company assigns to you.

How Long Do I Have to File a Personal Injury Lawsuit in Stockton?

For many California personal injury cases, California Code of Civil Procedure § 335.1 provides a two-year statute of limitations for claims involving injury or death caused by another person's negligence or recklessness. However, the deadline can change depending on the defendant and the type of claim.

What if a Government Agency Is Responsible?

If a government agency caused the accident, you can file a claim against it, but these claims have much shorter deadlines.

Under the California Government Claims Act, a claim involving personal injury or property damage must be presented to the appropriate government agency within six months. The California Courts also note that if the agency rejects the claim, the claimant has six months from the mailing of the rejection to file a lawsuit. Because exceptions and alternative deadlines may apply, you should not assume you automatically have two years to act.

Do I Have to Go to Court for a Personal Injury Claim?

You may not need to go to court, because many personal injury claims are resolved through negotiation before trial. However, if the parties cannot agree on compensation and liability, the case may go to trial, where both sides present their case to a judge and jury. When that happens, having a local personal injury lawyer represent your interests gives you a fighting chance at the justice you deserve.

How Can a Stockton Personal Injury Lawyer Help?

A personal injury lawyer can take much of the burden off injury victims by handling the legal process of their claim, making sure everything is filed correctly and all procedures are followed. An attorney can also investigate the circumstances of the accident and determine what evidence is needed to establish liability and damages.

Depending on the case, a Stockton personal injury lawyer may:

  • Investigate how the accident happened
  • Identify potentially liable parties
  • Obtain accident reports
  • Locate witnesses
  • Request surveillance or dashcam footage
  • Preserve evidence before it disappears
  • Review insurance policies
  • Calculate current and future damages
  • Consult medical or accident reconstruction experts
  • Communicate with insurance adjusters
  • Negotiate a settlement
  • File a lawsuit when appropriate
  • Prepare the case for trial

The goal of a personal injury lawsuit is not simply to prove that an accident happened. A successful claim must connect another party's conduct to the injuries and establish the resulting losses. If you are unfamiliar with the legal process, an experienced attorney can guide you through it and help you pursue the compensation you deserve.
